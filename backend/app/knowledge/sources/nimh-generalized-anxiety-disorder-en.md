Normal worry and anxiety disorders (NIMH). "Occasional anxiety is a normal part of life." "While occasional stress and anxiety are normal, they shouldn't control your life." NIMH explains that generalized anxiety disorder is not the same as occasionally worrying about things or feeling anxious because of stressful life events: "If anxiety doesn't go away and begins to interfere with your daily activities, you may have an anxiety disorder."

Common signs of generalized anxiety (NIMH). People with generalized anxiety disorder may worry excessively about everyday things, have trouble controlling their worries or feelings of nervousness, feel irritable or "on edge", feel restless or have trouble relaxing, have a hard time concentrating, have trouble falling or staying asleep or feel tired, and have aches such as headaches, muscle aches, stomachaches or tension. This list is general information and cannot be used to diagnose anyone.

Finding help for anxiety (NIMH). "If you have concerns about your mental health, talk to a primary care provider." NIMH says it is time to seek professional help if anxiety starts to cause problems in everyday life.

Looking after yourself with anxiety (NIMH). NIMH suggests learning about the signs and options for help, talking honestly with someone you trust about how you are feeling, and being "kind and patient with yourself and others". It also suggests recognising your progress and expecting that managing anxiety can include setbacks.
