What an emergency fund is (CFPB). An emergency fund is a cash reserve that's specifically set aside for unplanned expenses or financial emergencies. Some common examples include car repairs, home repairs, medical bills, or a loss of income. By putting money aside, even a small amount, for these unplanned expenses, you're able to recover quicker and get back on track towards reaching your larger savings goals.

How much to save (CFPB). The amount you need to have in an emergency savings fund depends on your situation. Think about the most common kind of unexpected expenses you've had in the past and how much they cost. This may help you set a goal for how much you want to have set aside. If you're living paycheck to paycheck, putting any money aside can feel difficult. But, even a small amount can provide some financial security.

Create a savings habit (CFPB). Set a goal: having a specific goal for your savings can help you stay motivated. Create a system for making consistent contributions; aim to make it a specific amount. Regularly monitor your progress: finding a way to watch your progress can offer gratification and encouragement to keep going. Celebrate your successes.

Manage your cash flow (CFPB). Your cash flow is essentially the timing of when your money is coming in (your income) and going out (your expenses and spending). If the timing is off, you can find yourself running short at the end of the week or month, but if you're actively tracking it, you'll start to see opportunities to adjust your spending and savings.

Make your saving automatic (CFPB). Saving automatically is one of the easiest ways to make your savings consistent so you start to see it build over time. One common way to do this is to set up recurring transfers so money is moved automatically from your checking account to your savings account. It's a good idea to be mindful of your balances, however, so you don't incur overdraft fees.
