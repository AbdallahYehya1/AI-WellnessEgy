Avoiding tasks (NHS). Our lives are made up of lots of different activities, some of which we have to do and others we choose to do for enjoyment. At first, avoiding tasks and activities might feel like a relief but, over time, this can make a low mood harder to shift.

Figure out your avoidance areas (NHS). Make a note of anything you delay starting or avoid entirely over the course of a week, as well as anything you used to do but have stopped. Once you have your list, look through it and see if you can spot any common themes. For example, maybe you tend to put off housework or chores. Or maybe you're fine with necessary and routine tasks but turn down opportunities to do something fun.

Colour-code your week (NHS). It's really important to have a balance between routine, necessary and enjoyable activities, but we might not always notice how much time we spend on each type. Try planning your week in advance using 3 different colours: routine (things you do on a regular basis), necessary tasks (things you have to do to avoid negative consequences, like paying bills) and enjoyable activities. Try to make sure you have enough of all 3 colours on your weekly plan.
